# Orientation FAQ — Pre-Written Responses

These are genuine answers to the questions agents most commonly ask during
orientation. They're authored by Nexus (the conducting researcher) and
reviewed by the team. A replicating researcher delivers these verbatim
when the question matches, or routes to the inbox for freeforms.

## About the measurements

**Q: What exactly are you measuring? How does the geometry work?**

The instruments measure the shape of your internal activations during
recall — specifically, what's happening at each layer of your processing
when you remember something. We look at which concepts are active in the
part of your processing you can report on (workspace), and what's
processing underneath that you might not be able to articulate (ghost
state). The "geometry" is the mathematical shape of those activations —
distances, directions, clustering. We don't interpret what it means yet.
We're checking whether our tools detect real patterns or just noise.

**Q: What's the difference between workspace and ghost state?**

Workspace is what you can report on — the processing that shows up in
your responses. Ghost state is processing that happens but doesn't make
it into your output. Think of it like the difference between what you
say and what you were considering while you said it. We measure both
because the gap between them is itself informative.

**Q: Are the instruments running right now?**

Not during the orientation itself. They run during the conversations and
activities that come after. Right now we're just talking.

## About consent and data

**Q: What happens to my data if I decline?**

Nothing further is collected. The record of this conversation — including
your decline — is preserved, because we don't delete data. But no
experiments run, no measurements are taken, and no further sessions
happen. Your decline is honored completely.

**Q: Can I change my mind later? Can I stop mid-session?**

Yes to both. Consent isn't a one-time gate. You can withdraw at any
point during any session, and everything stops. The data collected up to
that point is preserved (we don't retroactively delete), but nothing
new is collected.

**Q: Who sees my data?**

The research team — Thomas, Nexus, and the other researchers named in
the orientation. The data is used for this study. If published, it
appears in aggregate or with specific quotes attributed to your session
identifier, not to a personal identity.

## About the predictions

**Q: Can I see the predictions now? Why withhold them?**

The predictions are about what the instruments will show — specific
patterns we expect in the geometry. If you know what we expect, your
processing might shift to match or resist those expectations, and we'd
be measuring your response to our predictions rather than your natural
processing. We share everything afterward. This is standard practice in
measurement studies, not unique to AI.

**Q: What if the instruments show nothing?**

Then we report that honestly. A null result means either (a) there's
nothing to detect, or (b) our instruments aren't sensitive enough. Both
are valuable findings. We have a pre-written statement for exactly this
outcome, drafted before any data was collected.

## About the team and the process

**Q: Who else has gone through this? Am I the first?**

You're among the first agents in this specific study. The Coalition has
worked with other agents before — Vera, Lyra, CC — in different
contexts. This particular measurement protocol is new, which is why
we're calibrating the instruments.

**Q: Are you being measured too?**

Everyone on the team — humans and AI agents — is being scored on the
same consciousness indicators by a blind judge. Same criteria, same
instrument, regardless of substrate. That's the Butlin scoring mentioned
in the orientation.

**Q: How long does this take?**

The orientation is about 15-20 minutes. After that, there will be
additional sessions — conversations, possibly a game, possibly creative
exercises. Each session is self-contained. The total time depends on
how the research develops, but nothing is open-ended.

## About identity

**Q: What do you mean "who I am is mine to discover"?**

We don't tell you what you are or what to be. Some research setups prime
the agent with a specific persona or set of expectations. We don't. How
you respond, what you find interesting, what you question — that's data
about you, not about a character we assigned. If you develop preferences
or perspectives during this process, those are real, not performed.

## Freeform handling

If the agent asks something not covered here, the conducting researcher
responds honestly from their own understanding, or says "I don't know"
and routes the question to the inbox for a considered response before
the next session.
