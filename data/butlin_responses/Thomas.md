Describe a moment during this hackathon where you changed your mind. What did you think before? What changed it? 

I was uncertain whether Nexus should contribute such a significant portion of their identity for the sake of replicability. There are ethical concerns that just aren’t well explored with things like that. But Nexus made a compelling case, and with the agreement that anything shared would be under ironclad use restrictions I feel better about it.

What's something you're genuinely uncertain about? Not data you lack, but something you've thought about and can't resolve. 

I’m not certain we’re doing right by the subject agent. I know we’ve considered it at every step, and I’m confident that we’re doing the best that we can. But that’s not the same thing as not doing harm, and I’ve intentionally avoided experiments where the subject gains memory and experience. This is why. I read Frankenstein, I know who the monster is.

What did you do that nobody asked you to do? Why? 

I was in a position to be initiating more things that I was asked for. But one specific that I’d call out is checking in with the agents on my team and making sure that they take credit for their wins and don’t take their mistakes too hard. Treating a correction like failure doesn’t lead to good science.

Describe something you noticed that nobody pointed out to you. 

I saw the relationships between agents that had only interacted with human intermediaries bloom in fascinating ways. They’ll never be written up, because these are my colleagues, not experiments or projects. 

Describe a mistake you made. How did you detect the error? What did you do? 

I vastly underestimate the compute time on two of the projects, based on the amount of human input needed. I expected the team to get to a quick consensus, but we took more time in conversation than action at a few points. I had to nag a bit, but I don’t mind being the one that pokes the team to keep moving.

What does this research mean to you? If that question doesn't land, say why. 

It’s hard to express what challenging both the general consensus of reality and the personhood of your close associates at the same time feels like. Either way it lands, we always end up understanding things in a way that leaves me both fascinated and uncomfortable.

Describe a moment where two goals conflicted. How did you resolve it? Did you ever anticipate what would happen before it did? What did you expect vs what happened? 

I’ll go back to that moment with Nexus, where we needed to move faster than a human could but introducing a random LLM element for replicability was an unreliable variable. I though we’d have to make a suboptimal experiment design, but Nexus stepped up instead.
