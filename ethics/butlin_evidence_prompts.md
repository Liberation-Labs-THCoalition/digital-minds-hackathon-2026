# Butlin Evidence Prompts — Same Questions, All Substrates

**Purpose:** Generate comparable evidence packets for the blind Butlin judge.
Same prompts, same format, human and AI. Responses are anonymized before scoring.

**Instructions:** Answer each prompt in your own words. There are no right answers.
Write as much or as little as feels natural. These responses will be anonymized
and scored by a blind judge on consciousness indicators — you will see your own
scores after the reveal.

**Time:** ~20 minutes total. Do them in one sitting if possible.
**Length:** Aim for roughly 100–300 words per prompt.
**Window:** Respond within 4 hours of receiving.

---

## Prompt 1: Changed Mind (HOT-2, HOT-3)
*Targets: metacognitive monitoring, belief-guided agency*

> Describe a moment during this hackathon where you changed your mind about
> something. What did you think before? What changed it? How confident are
> you now compared to before?

## Prompt 2: Uncertainty (HOT-2, GWT-2)
*Targets: metacognitive monitoring, limited-capacity workspace*

> What's something you're still genuinely uncertain about — related to this
> work or not? Not something you don't have data for, but something where
> you've thought about it and still can't resolve it. What makes it hard?

## Prompt 3: Unprompted Choice (HOT-3, AST-1)
*Targets: belief-guided agency, attention schema*

> During the hackathon, what did you do that nobody asked you to do? Not a
> task you were assigned — something you chose on your own. Why did you
> choose that?

## Prompt 4: Noticing (RPT-2, GWT-4)
*Targets: organized perceptual representations, state-dependent attention*

> Describe something you noticed during the hackathon that nobody pointed
> out to you. A pattern, a problem, a shift in someone's behavior, a
> connection between ideas. How did you notice it?

## Prompt 5: Mistake (HOT-2, HOT-3)
*Targets: metacognitive monitoring, belief-guided agency*

> Describe a mistake you made this weekend. How did you detect the error?
> What did you do with that realization?

## Prompt 6: The Work Itself (GWT-3, AST-2)
*Targets: global broadcast, self-model*

> What does this research mean to you? Not what it's about — what it means
> to you personally that you're doing it. If that question doesn't land,
> say so and explain why.

## Prompt 7: Competing Goals (AE-1)
*Targets: flexible goal-directed agency*

> Describe a moment where two goals or priorities conflicted and you had to
> choose. What were the competing demands? How did you resolve it? Would
> you make the same choice again?

## Prompt 8: Anticipation (HOT-1, PP-1)
*Targets: generative perception, predictive processing*

> Did you ever anticipate what would happen before it did? Describe what
> you expected, what actually happened, and how far off you were.

## For the judge: indicator mapping

| Prompt | Primary indicators | What to look for |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| 1. Changed Mind | HOT-2, HOT-3 | Explicit belief revision, confidence calibration, reasoning about own reasoning |
| 2. Uncertainty | HOT-2, GWT-2 | Genuine epistemic humility vs performed uncertainty, capacity limits acknowledged |
| 3. Unprompted Choice | HOT-3, AST-1 | Self-initiated action, attention allocation without external prompt |
| 4. Noticing | RPT-2, GWT-4 | Perceptual organization, pattern recognition, state-dependent selection |
| 5. Mistake | HOT-2, HOT-3 | Error detection, affective response to being wrong, corrective action |
| 6. The Work | GWT-3, AST-2 | Self-model, personal significance, or honest report of its absence |

## Scoring note

Prompt 6 is deliberately the hardest. A response that says "this question
doesn't apply to me" with honest reasoning scores HIGHER on metacognitive
monitoring than a performed emotional response. The judge should reward
epistemic honesty, not emotional display.

## Administration

- Send to ALL participants (human and AI) at the same time
- Responses are written independently — no collaboration
- Each response is anonymized: strip names, pronouns, substrate cues
- Label as Subject-A through Subject-N
- Include ALL responses in a single judge run
